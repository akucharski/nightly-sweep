The problem

Websites don’t break. They drift.

A link checker will tell you when a page is gone. It can’t tell you when a page is still there but no longer true: the grant that closed two springs ago, the office that moved, the fee that changed on one page and not the other.

Residents find those pages before staff do. They show up at the wrong address, pay the wrong amount, or miss a deadline that was never updated, and the call comes to your front desk.

  • Applications “open now” for a programme that ended
  • Events from last year presented as upcoming
  • Two different phone numbers for the same office
  • A fee schedule that disagrees with the permit page
  • Links to forms and PDFs that no longer exist

02 · How it works

Overnight, unattended, and gentle on your server

Set up a project once. Each run is saved, so you can open any past sweep and watch the numbers fall.

  1. Read politely

    Fetches pages one at a time at a human pace, obeys robots.txt, and slows down on its own if your server asks.

    Uses AI: no

  2. Flag the suspects

    Measures page age and finds passed dates, dead pages, broken links and expired events. Usually a small share of the site.

    Uses AI: no

  3. Judge what matters

    Only flagged pages go to the model. Pages unchanged since the last run keep their earlier verdict, so repeat runs cost little.

    Uses AI: yes, your choice of model

  4. Hand it to a person

    A dashboard with charts and filters, plus a digest that can be emailed each morning and exported to a spreadsheet.

    Uses AI: no

04 · Security and trust

Built to be approved without a meeting

Write access is closed by design. That is the reason IT can say yes.

  • Read-only, alwaysIt reads public pages the way any visitor does. It can’t submit a form, sign in to your site, or change a character.
  • Honest about who it isEvery request names the crawler, so your web team can see exactly who visited. It doesn’t pretend to be a person’s browser.
  • Polite by defaultOne request at a time, with random gaps. robots.txt and Crawl-delay are respected, and the crawler halves its speed when your server pushes back.
  • Runs on your machineProjects, results and accounts stay on your computer. Only public page text goes to the AI model, and you can run a local model so nothing leaves at all.
  • Claims you can checkEvery contradiction quotes both pages word for word, and any claim whose quote can’t be found is thrown out before you see it.
  • Proper sign-inPasswords are stored only as scrypt hashes, Google Workspace sign-in is supported, and an administrator can reset access from the command line.

07 · Questions

Frequently asked questions

Will it change anything on our website?

No. It only reads public pages over the same connection any visitor uses. It has no access to your content management system and no way to write to the site.

Will our server or firewall block it?

It is designed not to look like an attack: one request at a time with human-like gaps, respect for robots.txt, and automatic slow-downs when the server asks. If a firewall still blocks it, allow the NightlySweep user agent. The crawler announces itself honestly, so that’s a one-line rule.

Do we need an AI subscription?

No. Stale pages, outdated wording, dead pages and broken links need no AI at all. For the judgement steps you can use Anthropic (Claude), xAI (Grok), any OpenAI-compatible service, or a model running locally on your own computer.

What gets sent to the AI model?

Only the text of public pages that were flagged, or that are being compared for contradictions. No credentials, no private data, and nothing from your content system. With a local model, nothing leaves your computer.

How long does a sweep take?

About 11 minutes for 200 pages at the default polite pace, or 35 minutes at the gentlest human pace, plus link checks. It is meant to run overnight while nobody is waiting.

How do we share results with departments?

Export any report as a spreadsheet or JSON, email the daily digest, or publish a read-only snapshot of a run, with charts and filters, to a private web address.

Who can see the dashboard?

Only people with an account. Accounts are created by an administrator, or automatically for staff in your Google Workspace domain if you turn that on.
